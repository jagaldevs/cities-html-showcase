HUMAN LANDSCAPES: CITIES — interactive HTML lesson
Haileybury Rendall · Year 8 Geography · Digital Innovation showcase
=====================================================================

OPEN IT
  Double-click index.html. It opens in any modern browser (Edge, Chrome,
  Safari, Firefox) and works offline. Fonts, images and code are all in
  the assets folder; nothing loads from the internet except the two
  YouTube videos (when you press play), the Canvas task-sheet link and
  the optional live class board.

CONTROLS  (press ? in the deck to see these)
  Move
    → / Space / Page Down    next slide            ← / Page Up   previous slide
    O                        overview: zoom out to every slide, click one to jump there
    M                        contents menu by section
    Home / End               first / last slide     Swipe on touch screens
  Present
    P                        presenter view in a second window: current and next slide,
                             speaker notes, lesson timer and clock. Drag it to your laptop
                             screen and put the deck on the projector. (Allow pop-ups.)
    F                        full screen
    A                        reveal / hide all blurred answers (or click one answer)
    H                        hide / show the pink ⚡ showcase tags
    R                        read the current slide aloud (press again to stop)
  Classroom tools
    D                        draw (pens, highlighter, undo; ink stays with each slide)
    L                        laser pointer        C   clear ink on this slide
    T                        countdown timer (1, 2, 3, 5 or 10 minutes, chimes at zero)
    N                        random name picker (paste your class list once; it is saved)
    J                        learning journal: every answer typed in the deck, ready to
                             save as PDF ("Save as PDF" printer) and upload to Canvas
    Esc                      close any tool

  Keys are ignored while you are typing in an answer box.

THE WOW MOMENTS
  Interactive maps (drag to spin, scroll or pinch to zoom, click for figures):
  Slide 6   Share of people living in urban areas: every country shaded by % urban on a
            3D globe. Press Play to watch 1950 → 2050, or drag the slider. Click a country
            for its figures. "Original map" shows the faculty's Our World in Data map.
  Slide 7   Why are cities located where they are? Spin the night-time Earth; the numbered
            pins fly the camera to each place.
  Slide 9   How cities are organised: click a zone in the legend to light it up across the
            real Melbourne map, with its share of the mapped land.
  Slide 18  Megacities rising: the 30 largest cities grow as spikes of light, 1950 → 2035,
            with a live top-10 leaderboard (Bengaluru is the gold spike).
  Slide 23  From space to street: the globe flies to Bengaluru; then spin it yourself.
  Slide 25  A city that kept growing: Bengaluru's growth 1950 → 2035 (dashed = projected).
  Data: UN World Urbanization Prospects 2018 (via Our World in Data); boundaries Natural Earth.

  The globes use WebGL (the graphics card), so they are smoothest on a modern laptop.
  Slides 11→12 and 26→27: elements morph between slides (Edge and Chrome).
  Slide 21  Quick check → "Team mode": a timed game show for 2–4 teams with scores,
            sound effects and confetti. Award points with the buttons or keys 1–4.
  Slide 26  Two Bengalurus → "City sounds": drag the divider to mix a traffic soundscape
            with the settlement's birds, water and pressure cookers (generated live,
            no audio files).
  Slide 28  Each character's 🔊 button reads their words aloud (uses the computer's
            built-in voices, so it works offline on Windows).
  Slide 4   Live class answers: people scan the QR code and add ideas on their phones,
            and a word cloud builds on the board. This one needs internet and a claude.ai
            sign-in. Share the board from its Share menu as Contributor. As the owner you
            can click a word to hide it, or clear the board between classes.

LINK TO A SLIDE
  The slide number is in the address bar: index.html#24 opens slide 24.
  Use it to link straight to an activity from a Canvas page.

STUDENT WORK
  Typed answers, sorts, quiz scores and mind-map ideas save automatically
  in that browser on that device (nothing is sent anywhere). The Exit
  reflection slide has a "Copy for Canvas" button that copies the answers
  to paste into a Canvas reply. To clear saved work, clear the browser's
  site data for this page.

EMBED IN CANVAS
  Upload the whole folder (keep index.html next to the assets folder) to
  Canvas Files, then embed index.html in a page with an iframe, e.g.
  <iframe src="…/index.html" width="100%" height="600" allowfullscreen></iframe>

SOURCES
  Every word of the source material is in the deck: Cities Introduction,
  Cities Dynamics Overview and Cities Management Sustainability (PowerPoints),
  the How Is Melbourne Organised activity (PDF), the UN Goal 11 page, and the
  Bengaluru inquiry paragraph. A script checked all 2,053 source words. Each
  slide footer names its source. Content written for this deck (Bengaluru
  case-study notes, facilitator prompts, suggested answers, quiz questions) is
  labelled as such in the footer. The Bengaluru people are composite voices,
  not real people, and the illustrations are stylised drawings.

  Film companion questions were written from the program's five chapters,
  not from the Canvas task sheet. Check the suggested answers against the
  program before relying on them.
